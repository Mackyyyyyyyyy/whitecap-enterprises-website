A city of two eras and two kinds of insulation needs

American Fork sits at the foot of Mount Timpanogos in the Wasatch Range and has grown from about 7,700 residents in 1970 to over 33,000 today. That growth shows up as two very different housing stocks in the same city: a historic downtown core dating back to the 1850s (the old city hall is on the National Register of Historic Places), and newer subdivisions built mostly after the 1970s growth boom.

With a 71% homeownership rate, American Fork is full of long-term owners who invest in their homes rather than move on quickly, which means insulation and garage door upgrades are usually about doing it right once, not the cheapest quick fix. Older homes near the historic core often have outdated, settled, or missing insulation and garage doors well past their service life, while even the newer neighborhoods benefit from an upgrade given how much Utah's seasonal temperature swings ask of attic insulation and door hardware.

We work on both ends of that spectrum: retrofitting older American Fork homes and finishing out newer ones.
