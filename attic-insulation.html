Why the attic matters most

Heat rises, and an under-insulated attic is usually the single biggest source of winter heat loss and summer heat gain in a house. Utah's residential energy code (2021 IECC as adopted for Climate Zone 5-B, which covers Utah County and the Salt Lake area) calls for a ceiling R-value of R-49. A lot of homes built in the 1980s, 90s, or early 2000s were insulated to older standards of R-30 or even R-19, which leaves real money leaking out through the roof every month.

We start every attic job by measuring existing depth and coverage, since insulation performs only as well as it's installed. Gaps around the attic hatch, compressed spots near storage areas, and thin coverage at the eaves are common problems that quietly cut a home's effective R-value well below what's actually up there.

Depending on your attic's access and existing insulation, we'll recommend either loose-fill (blown-in cellulose or fiberglass) for full, even coverage into every nook, or batts where the attic floor is regular and easy to access. Either way, we insulate to full code depth without blocking the soffit vents that let your roof breathe.

Signs your attic needs an upgrade

If any of these sound familiar, your attic insulation is probably underperforming:

·Heating and cooling bills that feel high for your home's size
·Ice dams forming at the roof edge in winter
·Upstairs rooms that run hot in summer, cold in winter
·A home built before the 2000s with original insulation
FAQ

Attic insulation FAQ

How do I know if my attic needs more insulation?
Common signs include high energy bills, rooms that never feel the right temperature, ice dams in winter, and being able to see the tops of your ceiling joists. If your insulation looks thin, uneven, or old, it is worth having it checked.
What is the best type of attic insulation?
It depends on your attic and budget. Blown-in insulation is excellent for covering an attic floor evenly, while spray foam is ideal when you want to air-seal the roofline. We will recommend the best fit after looking at your space.
How much attic insulation do I need in Utah?
For Utah's climate, attic insulation in the range of R-49 to R-60 is generally recommended. During your free estimate we measure what you have now and let you know how much to add to reach an efficient level.
Will new attic insulation lower my energy bills?
In most cases, yes. The attic is where a large share of heating and cooling escapes, so bringing it up to the right level is one of the most cost-effective upgrades for year-round comfort and lower bills.
