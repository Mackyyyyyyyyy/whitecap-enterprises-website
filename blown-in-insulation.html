Insulating without the demo

A lot of older homes across Pleasant Grove, American Fork, and the surrounding area were built with little or no wall insulation at all, and tearing out finished drywall just to insulate isn't something most homeowners want to sign up for. Dense-pack blown-in insulation solves that: we drill a small access hole per stud bay (usually from the exterior siding or interior wall), blow loose-fill material in under pressure until the cavity is fully packed, then patch the hole.

Loose-fill cellulose, made from recycled paper treated with borate for fire and pest resistance, is a common choice for dense-pack wall retrofits and has good sound-dampening properties as a side benefit. Loose-fill fiberglass is another option, often used for attic floors where it settles into an even blanket across joists, eaves, and awkward framing that batts can't fully cover.

Because it's blown rather than cut and fitted, blown-in insulation is also the fastest way to get complete, gap-free coverage across an irregular attic floor, which is why we often recommend it over batts for attics with a lot of obstructions, low clearance areas, or non-standard framing.

·Existing finished walls with little or no insulation
·Attics with irregular framing, low clearance, or lots of obstructions

FAQ

Blown-in insulation FAQ

What is blown-in insulation?
Blown-in insulation is loose-fill material, usually cellulose or fiberglass, that is blown into attics and wall cavities with a machine. It settles into place and fills gaps and hard-to-reach spots that batts often miss.
Is blown-in insulation better than batts?
For attic floors and irregular spaces, blown-in often provides more complete, gap-free coverage than batts because it conforms around framing, wiring, and obstructions. Batts still make sense in many wall applications.
Can you add blown-in insulation over my existing insulation?
In most cases, yes. If your current insulation is dry and in good condition, we can add blown-in material on top to bring the attic up to an efficient depth. If it is damaged or contaminated, we will discuss removal first.
How long does a blown-in insulation job take?
Many attic projects are completed in a single day, depending on the size of the space and how much material is needed. We will give you a clear timeline with your estimate.
