Fiberglass Insulation

Fiberglass batt insulation, installed without shortcuts

Fiberglass batts remain one of the most reliable, cost-effective ways to insulate walls, floors, and attics, as long as they're cut and fitted correctly, without the gaps and compression that quietly cut their R-value.

A proven material, done right

Fiberglass batts run roughly R-3.1 to R-3.8 per inch depending on density, and come in standard widths sized for 16-inch and 24-inch on-center framing. Utah's Zone 5B energy code calls for wall insulation of R-20 cavity or R-13+5 continuous, which typically means R-19 or R-21 batts in a 2x6 wall, or R-13 in a 2x4 wall paired with continuous exterior insulation.

The single biggest factor in how fiberglass actually performs isn't the R-value printed on the bag. It's the installation. Batts that are compressed to fit a cavity, split around wiring and boxes without patching the gap, or left with air pockets at the edges can lose a meaningful share of their rated performance. We cut every batt to fit its actual cavity, split it cleanly around obstructions, and make sure facing (where used) is installed on the correct side for your climate.

Because it's a dry, non-expanding material, fiberglass is also a practical choice for retrofit work in open wall cavities, floors over unconditioned crawl spaces or garages, and attic floors where batts are a better fit than loose-fill.

FAQ

Fiberglass insulation FAQ

What is fiberglass insulation?
Fiberglass insulation is made from fine glass fibers and comes as batts, rolls, or loose fill. It is one of the most widely used and cost-effective insulation materials for walls, attics, floors, and basements.
Is fiberglass insulation a good choice?
Yes, when it is installed correctly. Fiberglass is affordable and effective at slowing heat transfer, but proper fit with no gaps or compression is what makes the difference. Our crews install it to perform the way it should.
Where is fiberglass insulation typically used?
Fiberglass batts are commonly used in wall cavities, attic floors, floors over unconditioned space, and basement or crawl space walls. It is a versatile option for both new construction and retrofits.
Fiberglass or spray foam, which is better?
Fiberglass is the more budget-friendly option and works well in standard framing, while spray foam costs more but also air-seals. The right choice depends on your goals and budget, which we are happy to walk through during a free estimate.
