Built for Lehi's growth

Lehi has grown from about 47,000 residents in 2010 to well over 90,000 today, a 60%-plus jump in a decade that's made it the literal population center of Utah. That growth is powered by the "Silicon Slopes" tech corridor running through the city, home to major employers like Adobe, Microsoft, and Ancestry.com, alongside historic anchors like the Lehi Roller Mills and Thanksgiving Point.

With nearly 21,000 housing units and vacancy under 4%, most of Lehi's homes are new construction in fast-built subdivisions. New doesn't always mean well-insulated or fitted with a quality garage door. Production builders often use the minimum spec, and it shows the first time a Lehi winter (lows in the high teens) or summer (highs near 90°F) really tests the house. For young, growing families (the median age here is just 26), upgrading insulation or a garage door is often about comfort, energy bills, and curb appeal all at once.

We work in Lehi's newer neighborhoods regularly, so we know what the builder-grade materials look like and where they tend to fall short.
