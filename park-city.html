Built for altitude, snow load, and Park City's mixed housing stock

Park City was born as a silver mining camp in the 1860s, and Main Street's 64 Victorian-era buildings, now on the National Register of Historic Places, are a reminder of just how old some of this housing stock really is. When the mines played out in the mid-1900s, the town reinvented itself around skiing, and today Park City Mountain Resort anchors one of the largest ski areas in the country. That history means we see everything from century-old miners' cottages to brand-new ski chalets and condos, often on the same block.

At 6,936 feet, Park City averages over 120 inches of snow a year, with winter lows regularly in the teens. That's a serious test for any building envelope, and it's especially unforgiving on older Victorian-era homes that were never built with modern insulation standards, or on vacation properties that sit unoccupied, and unmonitored, for stretches at a time. With well over half of the city's housing units used as seasonal or vacation homes rather than full-time residences, a poorly insulated attic or a garage door that isn't sealing right can go unnoticed for months, running up energy costs or letting in snow and moisture the whole time.

We insulate both historic Main Street-area properties and newer construction, and we install and repair garage doors built to handle real mountain snow load.
