What spray foam insulation does

Spray polyurethane foam is applied as a liquid that expands on contact, filling every crevice in a stud bay, rim joist, or crawl space before curing into a solid, adhered layer. Because it forms a continuous seal rather than a cut-to-fit piece, it stops air leakage in places that are otherwise almost impossible to insulate well: rim joists, foundation sill plates, plumbing and wiring penetrations, and oddly framed corners.

Closed-cell foam is the denser of the two types, delivering roughly R-6 to R-6.5 per inch and doubling as a vapor and moisture barrier, which is why it's the right call for crawl spaces, basement rim joists, and detached shops or outbuildings like the metal building shown here. Open-cell foam is lighter and less expensive per board foot, with an R-value closer to R-3.5 to R-4 per inch, and is typically used in interior wall cavities and attic undersides where a moisture barrier isn't needed.

Because spray foam requires specialized equipment, correct mixing ratios, and proper personal protective equipment during application, it's one of the few insulation types where professional installation isn't just recommended. It's the only safe way to get a consistent result.

Where spray foam makes the most sense

Spray foam earns its higher material cost in specific spots where air sealing matters as much as R-value:

·Rim joists and sill plates, a top source of drafts in older homes
·Crawl spaces and basement walls where moisture control matters
·Metal buildings, shops, and outbuildings with irregular framing
·Additions and remodels with hard-to-reach or oddly shaped cavities

FAQ

Spray foam insulation FAQ

What is spray foam insulation and how does it work?
Spray foam is applied as a liquid that expands on contact to fill cracks, gaps, and irregular framing that batts and blown-in materials cannot reach. As it cures it forms a continuous air seal, stopping the drafts and air leaks that drive up energy bills.
Is spray foam insulation worth the cost?
For many Utah homes, yes. Spray foam seals air leaks other materials miss, adds rigidity, and closed-cell foam doubles as a moisture barrier. That air sealing often means lower heating and cooling bills and a more comfortable, consistent indoor temperature.
What is the difference between open-cell and closed-cell spray foam?
Open-cell foam is lighter and a good sound dampener, while closed-cell foam is denser, delivers a higher R-value per inch, and acts as a vapor and moisture barrier. We recommend the right type based on where it is being installed.
Where can spray foam insulation be installed?
We apply spray foam in attics, walls, crawl spaces, rim joists, sheds, shops, and metal buildings across Northern and Central Utah, anywhere a strong air seal makes a difference.
